# Project naming brief — working draft

**Status:** Proposal for discussion; no name selected
**Author:** Christina / Hermes
**Date:** 2026-11-08

## Purpose

Develop a distinctive, easy-to-say and easy-to-spell name for the project without narrowing its scope or borrowing a living community's institutional name casually. This brief records a provisional view and a research direction, not a naming decision or availability clearance.

## Scope the name must fit

The project studies how people govern across historical and contemporary contexts, and how evidence about institutions and mechanisms may inform systems involving humans and AI. Its remit includes authority, collective decisions, accountability, dispute resolution, adaptation, and human flourishing. It is not only a study of one governance theory, a single historical tradition, or an AI product.

## Polycentra: retain as a candidate, not the umbrella concept

*Polycentra* is worth keeping on the shortlist: it is compact, pronounceable enough to test, and signals multiple centers. But it evokes **polycentric governance**, a specific and important body of scholarship, not governance in general. Polycentric arrangements involve multiple decision-making centers with some autonomy and consequential interaction or coordination; merely spanning multiple scales does not make a project or system polycentric. The Ostrom Workshop presents polycentric governance as a distinctive analytical framework and research tradition.[1]

That framework can inform Agora, but our research umbrella also covers institutional forms and questions that are not reducible to polycentricity. Making *Polycentra* the project name could therefore lead readers to expect an Ostrom-centered program. If retained, use a plain descriptor and explain that it is a working name, not a claim that the project's whole scope is polycentric governance. Test pronunciation, spelling from memory, and first-impression meaning with people outside the current group before deciding.

## Widen the naming research beyond the Greek canon

The repository's research map calls for comparative study across kinship and customary institutions, councils, legal orders, commons, colonial and postcolonial arrangements, and more.[2] Examples already represented in the working research include:

- **Oromo Gadaa:** a living governance tradition. UNESCO documents its role in political, economic, social, and religious life, rotational leadership, and its limits on class membership; a participant-scholar account discusses accountability and checks and balances. Include both design features and exclusions when studying it; do not treat it as a plug-in template.[3][4]
- **Quechua ayllu and Sumaq Kausay:** an Indigenous-led collaborative case study in Lares describes community governance, participation, deliberation, and stewardship in its own place-based context. Its authorship and community-research setting should remain visible when using it.[5]
- **Councils in British Africa:** a historical comparative study of more than 450 subnational units examines executive constraints and the interaction of local institutions, colonial administration, and pressure from below. The colonial setting is essential context, not a generic source of attractive branding.[6]

These examples suggest research questions and mechanism vocabulary—how authority is constrained, renewed, shared, and made answerable—not names to appropriate. A community's institution should not become a brand merely because its name is distinctive or searchable. Where a candidate draws directly on a living tradition, seek relevant community perspectives and assess whether using it is appropriate before advancing it.

## Naming criteria and next steps

1. Describe the project accurately in a short subtitle; avoid implying enforcement powers, certification, or an adopted governance model.
2. Prefer names that are short, easy to spell after hearing once, pronounceable across likely audiences, and not dependent on a specialist glossary.
3. Keep the umbrella broad enough for comparative human-governance research and applied human–AI work.
4. Draw inspiration from mechanisms and findings across regions and traditions; do not treat non-European names as a pool of branding material.
5. For each finalist, search exact and similar uses across web, GitHub, software registries, domains, and relevant trademark databases; record date and limits. Search silence is not clearance.
6. Test candidates with people outside the current group, including whether the name suggests the wrong theory, tradition, or product.

**Current position:** Keep *Polycentra* provisionally in consideration while continuing a broader, mechanism-led search. Do not select or announce a project name until the shortlist has been screened and tested.
