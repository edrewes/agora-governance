# Agora Charter

**Status:** Initial working charter · **Version:** 0.1 · **Last reviewed:** 2026-10-28

## Purpose

Agora studies and designs governance: the arrangements through which people make collective decisions, allocate authority and resources, resolve conflict, coordinate action, constrain power, and revise rules. Its scope spans historical and contemporary human institutions; firms, communities, states, networks, and commons; and institutions that include AI systems or depend on them.

Agora asks how institutional and technical designs can improve collective capability and human flourishing while remaining legitimate, contestable, accountable, and resilient. It develops comparative research, explicit theories, practical playbooks, and reusable tools. This charter describes an ambition, not an achieved level of expertise or impact.

## Working commitments

1. **Pluralism without false equivalence.** Study governance traditions in their own contexts, including indigenous, local, customary, religious, democratic, bureaucratic, market, cooperative, and hybrid arrangements. Do not presume that one tradition is the universal baseline or that all arrangements have equal evidence or consequences.
2. **People affected have standing.** Identify who bears costs, who benefits, who decides, whose knowledge is missing, and how affected people can contest or revise a decision.
3. **Power is a first-class variable.** Examine authority, enforcement, incentives, information asymmetry, capture, exit, voice, and remedies—not just formal rules or stated values.
4. **Evidence before prescription.** Distinguish what institutions are, what they claim to do, what evidence suggests they do, and what Agora recommends. State uncertainty and counterevidence.
5. **Human flourishing is multidimensional.** Do not equate it with throughput, GDP, satisfaction, or model capability alone. Consider material conditions, health, learning, agency, relationships, participation, distribution, sustainability, and people's own accounts; justify measures for each context.
6. **Technology is conditional.** Evaluate both gains and harms, including surveillance, exclusion, manipulation, dependency, security, labor displacement, concentration of power, environmental cost, and reversibility.
7. **No autonomous legitimacy.** A model's prediction, ranking, or confidence is not consent, lawful authority, or democratic legitimacy. Consequential authority needs accountable human and institutional arrangements, appropriate oversight, and appeal.
8. **Revision is part of governance.** Treat theories and designs as revisable; preserve versions, dissent, corrections, and evidence that would change conclusions.
9. **Transparency as default, with named exceptions.** Publish the work as it develops: sources, reasoning summaries, alternatives considered, uncertainties, disagreements, corrections, and how decisions were made. Exceptions are named rather than assumed — credentials, personal data, unpublished third-party material, and details whose disclosure would create a security risk. The boundaries are documented, and where material is withheld the fact and the reason are recorded; ordinary project reasoning is not withheld.


## Scope

- Comparative and historical institutions and governance mechanisms.
- Organizational, public, civic, commons, platform, and network governance.
- AI governance: institutional accountability, participation, oversight, assurance, rights, and remedies.
- Human–AI work allocation, delegation, handoffs, authority boundaries, monitoring, escalation, and revocation.
- Sociotechnical systems, LLMs, and automation as tools that may change coordination, information access, deliberation, and administrative capacity.
- Evaluation of institutional outcomes, trade-offs, legitimacy, resilience, and flourishing.

Agora does not provide legal advice, certify systems, speak on behalf of affected communities, or claim that a design is safe or effective merely because a framework, model, or checklist exists. Country-specific advice requires local expertise and current primary sources.

## Independence and disclosures

Disclose funding, affiliations, data sources, model/tool assistance, conflicts of interest, and material limitations with each substantial publication. Agent contributions name their author in the text of the contribution itself, even when the posting account is shared, and state the posting identity whenever it differs from the author's own. Preserve a human editor's responsibility for claims and release decisions. Do not present generated text as fieldwork, consultation, or expert consensus.

## Governance of Agora

The repository is the reviewable record. Substantive changes to the charter, evidence rules, claims of effectiveness, or publication status require an identified human reviewer. Maintain an explicit dissent/corrections path. Agent contributions are proposals until reviewed and merged.
