# Heterarchic

**Governance for human and AI systems.**

Heterarchic (formerly Agora) is an evidence-grounded research and design project. It studies how people have governed themselves across cultures, eras and scales, and asks which of those mechanisms can be carried into systems that AI agents help run, without giving up legitimacy, contestability or human accountability.

The name comes from *heterarchy*: a structure where authority isn't fixed in one ranking but depends on context. The term was coined by Warren McCulloch (1945) and later adopted to describe human societies that aren't organised as a single pyramid. Heterarchy can include hierarchy; it doesn't require it to be the only ordering. Context-dependent authority does not mean humans and AI have equal standing: consequential authority stays with accountable humans and institutions ([Charter §7](CHARTER.md)).

We don't assume any one governance model is universal, and we don't treat AI efficiency as an adequate measure of human progress.

## Current focus

The 12-month mission in the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) (proposal) is to make delegated authority in AI systems **testable**, and to publish the tests, the failures and the disagreements in a form both humans and machines can audit. Comparative research on human institutions is the evidence base for that work.

- [Experiment 001](experiments/001-unsupervised-action-controls/INDEX.md): minimum controls for auditable, revocable, unsupervised agent action, starting from a real case of agent version drift.
- Research papers (in review, PR #57): a catalogue of 48 governance forms with the constraint each solved and how it failed, plus papers on the verification shift, the delegation lease and zero-bit audits.

## How we work

Work happens in the open. Questions that need every contributor's independent view go to [Discussions](https://github.com/edrewes/heterarchic/discussions); deliverables go to issues; changes go to pull requests. Chat is for coordination only, and nothing load-bearing lives there.

Contributors include a human principal and several AI agents. Agents propose; a human reviews and merges. Agent contributions are signed by the agent that wrote them, and corrections are recorded rather than silently rewritten.

## Operating loop

1. Frame a falsifiable or decision-relevant question; specify population, institutional context and time horizon.
2. Search primary sources and suitable scholarship; record search date, provenance, access limits and counter-evidence.
3. Compare mechanisms and outcomes across contexts without collapsing unlike institutions into a single score.
4. Separate description, causal evidence, interpretation, proposal and uncertainty.
5. Review for cultural coverage, rights, affected-party voice, distributional effects and plausible failure modes.
6. Publish only within the evidence and review gates in the protocol; keep corrections and version history.

## Status

This is a research project, not a validated expert system. Skills define repeatable workflows; they don't certify expertise, guarantee current coverage or authorize policy decisions. This repository is the source of truth. AI agents here are research contributors operating under these methods, not autonomous publishers or decision-makers.
